Vela
Catch the whole field.

A panoramic camera that records the entire pitch, follows the play on its own, and renders match-quality footage overnight — on the device you already own.

01

The footage shouldn't be hostage

The footage belongs to whoever filmed it. So Vela never locks your match in someone else's cloud, and never ties you to a subscription. The night the match ends, the finished cut comes together in your hands.

02

What makes it Vela

I.

It sees the whole field

Two 4K lenses become one panorama. Within it, an AI virtual camera tracks the play on its own, so there's nobody behind the camera and nothing out of frame.

II.

It renders at home, overnight

No render farm, no cloud bill. Plug Vela in after the match and it processes the high-quality cut on its own silicon while it charges. The match-quality footage is waiting by morning.

III.

It's yours the rest of the week

A camera plays one match a week and sleeps the other six days. Vela doesn't. Between games it's a quiet edge-AI node in your home — a panoramic pair of eyes and real compute you actually own.

The subscription is small on purpose: it funds better stitching and analysis models and keeps your device in tune. The heavy lifting stays on hardware you've already paid for.

05

One sky, one family

LepusLab names its work after the constellations. Two of them tell the whole story.

Lepus · the hare

The watcher

A hare's eyes sit wide on its head — it sees almost all the way around without turning. That instinct to take in the whole field at once is where LepusLab starts.

Vela · the sails

The reach

Vela is Latin for "the sails." It carries that wide-eyed watching out into the open and unfurls it across the pitch — one frame, the entire game.

06

How Vela speaks

Plain over clever

Name what people control. "Render overnight," not "asynchronous post-processing pipeline."

Confident, not loud

State the difference once, clearly. Let the result do the boasting.

A little astronomical

Sails, stars, the whole field. The metaphor is there when it earns its place — never as decoration.

Ownership, always

It's your footage, your device, your compute. The language never hands that back to a cloud.
